# Operator decision workspace — UI brief

Frontend handoff, 29 September 2026. This page describes what goes in each part of the app. Use the locked scenario scope, action candidates, general safety metrics, and action-specific safety metrics as the detailed sources.

The app helps an operator review a proposed plan. It does not send instructions to the grid. A plan is actionable only when its required safety checks pass and its assets and permissions are confirmed.

## 1. Top bar: choose the view

Offer two views:

- National grid: all-island conditions and affected groups.
- Precise grid / site: a selected site, its connection, and the route that limits it.

Show the selected location, current time, assessment window, and data status in both views. A site view must still show any all-island limit that affects the decision.

Label the source clearly: Live, Historical demonstration, Planning case, or No live connection. Never present a demonstration as a live assessment.

## 2. Search bar: find the situation

Use a ticket-style search bar. The operator can pick a suggested situation or describe one in plain language.

After a match, show what the app thinks the limiting problem is. Let the operator review it before assessment. More than one limit may apply at once.

Search suggestions must come from the locked scope. Use the plain-language descriptions below on screen. T1–T4 and H1–H4 can remain internal IDs.

### Transmission Constraint

Offer four conditions:

- A route is overloaded while all equipment is in service.
- The route is safe now, but one trip would overload it.
- A route is overloaded during an existing outage.
- The route is safe during an outage, but one further trip would overload it.

For each, ask whether the affected output comes from one local area, several areas sharing a route, or a wide renewable group. Also record the named line, cable or transformer; whether an outage is planned or forced; and whether the problem is happening now or forecast. These are details of the four conditions, not extra cases. Transmission-line voltage is outside this scenario scope.

### High Frequency / Minimum Generation

Offer these situations under the four locked causes:

- Measured high frequency: lost interconnector export, sudden demand drop, or unexpected generation/import rise.
- Minimum conventional units: the rule binds in Ireland, Northern Ireland, or both.
- Reserve: upward reserve, or downward reserve if the effective policy requires it.
- Ramping: power needs to rise or fall over the relevant future window.

Several requirements may bind together. A forecast surplus with normal measured frequency is not a high-frequency event; keep it in intake until the limiting cause is known.

### SNSP

Offer one all-island SNSP-limit situation. Record what moved it toward the limit: more renewable output, less demand, more imports, less exports, or a combination. "Now" and "forecast" are time settings, not new cases.

If the app cannot establish the cause, show Cause unknown and the facts needed to resolve it. This is an intake state, not a new scenario.

## 3. Situation table: review the facts

Start with values from connected operational feeds where available. Let the operator edit or add a value.

Every row needs:

- Value and unit.
- Source and timestamp.
- Whether it is measured, forecast, inferred, or entered by the operator.
- A clear current / stale / missing / conflicting state.

Keep the current operating plan and instructions already in force in this table. Record operator edits and reassess the result after any change. Do not ask the operator for a reliable, current fact already supplied by a connected system.

Show only the extra inputs relevant to the situation:

| Family | Facts the table needs |
| --- | --- |
| Transmission | Network connections and equipment status; actual and studied flows; ratings; limiting asset; outage and credible-failure set. |
| High Frequency / Minimum Generation | Live frequency; online unit counts and minimum output; qualified reserve; ramp requirement and forecast net-load change. |
| SNSP | Wind/solar output, demand, signed imports and exports, effective SNSP limit, and stability results. |

The general safety metrics give the full input list.

## 4. Left panel: safety first

Put the overall safety result at the top: Pass, Fail, or Unknown. Show why, and name the missing evidence when the result is unknown.

Below it, show the relevant family checks. For each check, show its measured or studied value, effective limit, margin, worst time, source, and result. For transmission, also show the worst credible equipment failure. Recheck the whole action combination after a step changes. Safety results must come from a validated assessment, not a calculation made only in the browser.

| Family | General safety checks shown first |
| --- | --- |
| Transmission Constraint | Flow versus the correct rating on the limiting route and any newly affected route; worst flow after one credible failure (or one further failure during an outage); whether relief arrives before a breach. |
| High Frequency / Minimum Generation | Live frequency and trend plus delivered response; qualified unit count and technical minima by jurisdiction; deliverable reserve versus requirement by direction/service; deliverable ramp versus requirement at each deadline. Show every binding H requirement. |
| SNSP | All-island SNSP and margin to its effective limit; inertia, rate-of-change-of-frequency and other required stability results; worst result during the action and its rebound. |

Show other affected limits too. For example, charging may help a transmission route but use battery capacity promised for frequency service. A safe SNSP percentage alone does not prove the system is stable.

### Expandable action-specific checks

Place these under the general checks, grouped by proposed action step. Show only checks for actions in the current plan. Put the first go/no-go item in each bullet at the top of its expanded row.

Transmission actions:

- Paired generator redispatch: matched MW at each step; each unit's available range and ramp; any temporary balance or reserve gap.
- Switch or sectionalise: clearance for the exact switch sequence; breaker, protection, fault-current and islanding checks.
- Return equipment early or defer work: release by the asset owner and work party; fitness to energise; switching permission and earliest approved time.
- Charge local storage or add contracted demand: metered relief behind the bottleneck, available energy or load duration, connection after the studied trip and later rebound.
- Apply a local, shared, wide or outage WDT limit: correct group and members; new setpoints and incremental delivered MW after existing limits.
- Change interconnector transfer: accepted signed MW, link headroom, ramp, delivery time and other TSO agreement.

High Frequency / Minimum Generation actions:

- Fast generation reduction, charging or pumping: MW available within the required seconds; activation delay, ramp, output/charge limits and observed response.
- Emergency HVDC help after lost export: an approved response in the required direction; another link's status, headroom, armed mode, agreed MW and timing.
- Swap to a lower-minimum conventional unit: qualified unit count and inertia throughout the changeover, including incoming readiness.
- Replace a reserve provider: credited MW for the exact service and direction; response and sustain time, energy and other commitments.
- Commit an offline unit, storage or demand reduction for an upward ramp: start after notice, MW delivered at each required deadline and hold time.
- Charge a battery while keeping its frequency service: state of charge, room to charge, charge MW and remaining response headroom in both directions.

SNSP actions:

- Lower imports or increase exports: accepted signed transfer and delivery time; SNSP when it arrives and when the transfer returns to schedule.
- Charge, pump or add contracted demand while releasing renewables: added demand and renewable MW together, the full SNSP ratio, duration and rebound.
- Apply an all-island WDT limit: correct set, delivered incremental MW after local limits, and resulting measured-output SNSP.

An action-specific check adds to the family checks; it never replaces them. The documented emergency HVDC response in one direction does not by itself approve a high-frequency response in the other.

## 5. Right panel: proposed action plan

Show the main action first, then every supporting or parallel step. An operator should be able to answer:

- What exactly would be instructed?
- Which named asset or party would do it?
- Can the operator instruct it directly, or must someone else clear or accept it?
- When can it start, when does its effect arrive, and how long does it last?
- What MW change is expected at the limiting location?
- Which safety or permission check still blocks it?

Show dependencies between steps, such as one generator increasing only after another can reduce. Let the operator change a step or enter another combination. Keep the original proposal for comparison and rerun all affected checks.

Plan labels:

- Actionable: all required safety, availability, timing and permission checks pass.
- Conditional: a named agreement or deliverability check is still pending.
- Unsafe: a required check fails.
- Insufficient evidence: a required check is unknown.

These are proposed UI labels, not new TSO operating rules. Do not provide a control that silently sends an instruction.

Units: MW is a power change or route relief. MWh is energy over a stated time window. Do not label MW as "energy saved."

## 6. Below the panels: compare outcomes and benefits

Compare four columns over the same time window:

- Current plan: what is in force at the decision time, including active instructions.
- No new instruction: how that plan develops if nothing new is issued.
- Proposed plan: the full suggested combination.
- Operator alternative: the operator's own combination.

The no-new-instruction column is the baseline for claimed improvements. Put safety rows above money and energy. A plan with a failed or unknown required safety check cannot win because of its benefits. (EirGrid/SONI balancing principles, §§4.3–4.5.)

| Card or comparison row | What to show |
| --- | --- |
| Safety and response | Worst limit margin, Pass/Fail/Unknown, delivered relief MW, response time, and time to breach. |
| Remaining dispatch-down | Expected constrained and curtailed MWh under each evaluated plan, kept separate where possible. |
| Avoided dispatch-down | No-new-instruction MWh minus proposed-plan MWh, with uncertainty. A MW × time upper bound is not proven saved energy. |
| Site dispatch-down risk | Site probability and expected MWh only if a validated site model exists. Otherwise show "Not established" and label any national estimate as context. |
| Net system resource cost | Change in dispatch and activation costs, payments, charging, losses, degradation and rebound, with the cost perspective named. |
| Gross market opportunity | Eligible sold or exported MWh × applicable price, only when the route, product, deliverability and price are known. Label it an estimate, not TSO profit. |
| Net financial value | Agreed revenue or avoided cost less incremental costs, with the accounting perspective stated. Keep separate from system resource cost. |
| Carbon effect | Difference between modeled emissions under no new instruction and the proposal, including material import/export and charging effects; show method and uncertainty. |

Recovered renewable power does not automatically reduce cost or carbon. A gas-displacement estimate is only a stated assumption unless the displaced generation is modeled. SEAI uses different emissions-factor boundaries for different purposes.

If an input or model is missing, show Not established and why. Do not substitute zero or a precise-looking estimate. Current project outputs do not prove a site-level outcome or avoided dispatch-down from a proposed action.

## 7. Bottom drawer: show the evidence

Let the operator expand a plain-language explanation of the result:

- Data source and time for each important input, plus any operator edit.
- Rule and model versions; limits and credible failures used.
- Why each action was included, rejected, or left conditional.
- Assumptions, uncertainty, missing checks and the time the plan was assessed.

Keep these details available without forcing the operator to read them before seeing the safety result.

## 8. Build states and data handoff

The existing case review, action card, outcome panel, and guardrail table can be reused. The current workspace type mainly holds one action and a short guardrail list. It needs room for combinations, several binding conditions, action-specific checks, four comparison states and evidence.

The frontend should display a backend assessment, including its missing-data reasons. The checked-in evaluator currently demonstrates only a paired local renewable and flexible-demand/charging action. Other actions are descriptive; its modeled capture is an upper bound and its recommendation is empty. The January and network examples are not live SCADA or approved safety results.

| Data sent to the screen | Minimum content |
| --- | --- |
| Case | Locked scenario IDs, view/location, time window, active instructions, facts with source/time, and operator edits. |
| Plans | Main action, all steps, named executor, permission state, timing, MW effect and dependencies. |
| Safety checks | Pass/Fail/Unknown, value, limit, margin, worst time/failure, source and reason for each family and action check. |
| Comparisons and benefits | The four plan states, matched time windows, outcome values, method, uncertainty and reason when unavailable. |
| Evidence | Live/fixture status, feed freshness, policy and model versions, assumptions and audit ID. |

### Frontend acceptance checks

- Search covers only the locked transmission, H-family and single SNSP case. Multiple binding conditions can be shown together.
- Missing, stale or conflicting evidence remains Unknown. No required Unknown or Fail can appear as Actionable.
- Changing an action or input makes the old assessment stale until it is rerun.
- The site view keeps relevant all-island checks visible.
- Plans needing another party's agreement stay Conditional until acceptance is recorded.
- All four comparison columns use the same window and the current instructions.
- MW and MWh are distinct. Benefit cards show method and source, or Not established.
- Historical, planning and fixture data never carry the same badge as validated live results.

### Decisions before an operator-facing release

- Ask EirGrid/SONI to confirm effective rules, safety thresholds, study requirements and data freshness limits.
- Confirm the instruction or approval route for each action.
- Agree the live data and study interfaces, including SCADA, asset capability, outages, WDT and interconnector acceptance.
- Validate the avoided-energy, site-risk, cost and carbon methods before treating their values as decision-ready.
