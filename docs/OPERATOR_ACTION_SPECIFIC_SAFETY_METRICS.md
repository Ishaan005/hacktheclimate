# Action-specific safety metrics for the operator

**Working draft, 29 September 2026.** Apply these **extra go/no-go checks** to the relevant action candidates in [What the operator sees, and what we could suggest](OPERATOR_ACTIONS_AND_METRICS.md), after the [general family safety metrics](OPERATOR_GENERAL_SAFETY_METRICS.md). The three families and their situations remain exactly as in the [locked scope](OPERATOR_SCENARIO_SCOPE.md). Several actions recur in more than one situation; their extra checks recur too. The figures below are **measures to obtain**, not new operating thresholds. EirGrid/SONI must confirm the effective acceptance rule and the action's authority.

A family result can pass while the action is still unusable: the route may be thermally safe **if** a switch occurs, but the switch may be unavailable; the system may have enough reserve **if** a battery responds, but that battery may lack charge or be committed elsewhere. For a combination, check **each action's extra gates**, then rerun the family gates for the combination at every delivery time. A failed or unknown extra gate blocks a recommendation. This pass/unknown screen is **our proposed product design**; EirGrid/SONI's dispatch process uses current technical data, declarations and actual instructions. [Balancing Market Principles Statement (BMPS) v9, §§3.2 and 4.4](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Balancing_Market_Principles_Statement_Version_9.0.pdf).

## Transmission Constraint

| Action candidate in the existing list | Crucial **action-specific** measure and go/no-go question | Why the general thermal margin is insufficient | Evidence and data owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Studied switching or sectionalising** in shared or wide-route situations | **Executable topology:** actual breaker/isolator positions; authorised switching sequence; protection settings and fault-current duty in the *new* topology; any unintended island. Do switching and protection staff approve that exact sequence before relief is needed? | A power-flow result assumes the new topology exists. It does not prove that equipment can be switched or protected safely during the transition. | TSO/network-owner control and protection records, switching authority and updated security study. EirGrid separately requires equipment within short-circuit duty and protection to operate at minimum fault current. [Operating Security Standards, §5](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Operating-Security-Standards-Version-3.0-April-2025.pdf). The precise sequence check is **our proposed gate** for operator confirmation. |
| **Early return of an out-of-service line, cable or transformer**, or deferral of planned work | **Released-to-energise status:** fault cleared or work party withdrawn; required declaration of fitness/energisation instruction; earliest approved return time. Has the asset owner and work party actually released the equipment, and is it available before the overload or failure risk? | A study can show that a restored line would help even when workers remain on it or a forced fault is unresolved. | Network owner, outage coordinator and work party; current outage and switching record. EirGrid's transmission outage process involves planned work, studies and network technician resources. [EirGrid outage information](https://www.eirgrid.ie/industry/customer-information/outage-information). |
| **Paired cross-area generator redispatch** | **Matched deliverable MW trajectory:** exporting-side reduction and replacement-side increase by minute, including each unit's declared minimum/maximum and ramp. Is the pair deliverable without an interim imbalance or loss of reserve? | A final-state network study can miss the transition while one unit moves before the other. | TSO dispatch log and unit technical declarations; compare actual metered response with instructions. BMPS says technical data and availability declarations govern feasible dispatch. [BMPS v9, §§3.2 and 4.4](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Balancing_Market_Principles_Statement_Version_9.0.pdf). The minute-by-minute paired view is **our proposed display**. |
| **Local storage charging or contracted extra customer demand** to relieve an export route | **Connected relief MW and persistence:** metered consumption at the electrically relevant site; connection that survives the studied trip; available charging energy/load duration; later rebound. Is relief still present during the entire bottleneck, including after the relevant contingency? | A route margin calculated with assumed load is unsafe if that load cannot be instructed, disconnects with the trip, fills its battery or rebounds early. | Asset/customer status, contract and connection study. Ordinary demand-side-unit dispatch covers **reducing** demand, so extra demand needs a separate controllable arrangement. [EirGrid demand-side units](https://www.eirgrid.ie/grid/grid-codes-and-compliance-overview/grid-code-compliance-testing/dsu-setup-and-testing); [BMPS v9, battery dispatch](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Balancing_Market_Principles_Statement_Version_9.0.pdf). Post-trip persistence is **our proposed gate**. |
| **Local, shared, wide or outage-specific WDT renewable limit**, including a pre-limit | **Correct instruction and delivered MW:** operator-selected constraint group or all-island set, current members, each unit's active setpoint, present output, existing binding limits and telemetry quality. Does the *incremental* setpoint actually deliver the required reduction before the risk? | A thermal study may assume renewable MW falls at the right sites. WDT allocation and existing setpoints can make the incremental delivered MW different from a simple group total. | TSO Wind Dispatch Tool and farm telemetry. EirGrid says WDT uses operator-selected groups, real-time conditions and a setpoint method that also accounts for frequency response. [WDT constraint-group overview, Appendix 1](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Wind-Dispatch-Tool-Constraint-Group-Overview_1.pdf). |
| **Interconnector transfer change** proposed to relieve a wide route | **Agreed feasible transfer:** signed MW direction, available net transfer capacity, link status, operational ramp, earliest agreed delivery and other TSO acceptance. Is the *agreed* transfer the one used in the network study? | A hypothetical transfer can relieve the route but cannot be counted before the counterparty accepts it, or if transfer/ramp capacity is unavailable. | TSO interconnector schedule, owner and other TSO. BMPS describes coordinated trades and the other TSO's right to reject them; the LFC agreement sets operational ramp restrictions. [BMPS v9, §3.4.6](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Balancing_Market_Principles_Statement_Version_9.0.pdf); [LFC Block Operational Agreement, December 2024, Art. 3](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/LFCBOA%20-Load-Frequency-Control-Block-Operational-Agreement-for-Ireland-and-Northern-Ireland.pdf). |

## High Frequency / Minimum Generation

| Action candidate in the existing list | Crucial **action-specific** measure and go/no-go question | Why the general family metric is insufficient | Evidence and data owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fast generator reduction, storage charging or pumping** during measured high frequency | **Real response at the required seconds:** available downward MW at this moment, activation delay, actual ramp, generator technical minimum and storage charge headroom. Does response arrive before frequency worsens, and is it observed after instruction? | A predicted frequency recovery is conditional on the chosen assets actually reducing net supply fast enough. | High-rate frequency and unit telemetry, technical declarations and dispatch log. EirGrid/SONI use actual frequency deviation and initiate actions promptly. [LFC Block Operational Agreement, December 2024, Art. 5](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/LFCBOA%20-Load-Frequency-Control-Block-Operational-Agreement-for-Ireland-and-Northern-Ireland.pdf). |
| **Emergency HVDC assistance after lost export** | **Available-link and armed-service state:** identify the failed link; verify a *different* link's status, transfer headroom, approved emergency mode, agreed MW and expected return-to-schedule. Is a mode approved for this **high-frequency/export** direction, armed or accepted, and able to respond in the needed seconds? | General frequency metrics cannot make a failed link available or establish another TSO's emergency service. | Interconnector control room, other TSO and service record. The LFC agreement describes arming, cross-border coordination and return to schedule; its described low-frequency response does **not** itself establish availability in the high-frequency direction. [LFC Block Operational Agreement, December 2024, Art. 17](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/LFCBOA%20-Load-Frequency-Control-Block-Operational-Agreement-for-Ireland-and-Northern-Ireland.pdf). |
| **Lower-minimum conventional-unit swap** in Ireland, Northern Ireland or both | **Transition qualification:** incoming unit start/synchronisation time, proven services and location; outgoing unit desynchronisation time; qualified count and inertia at **every intermediate step**. Can the incoming unit support the required services before the outgoing unit leaves? | An end-state count can pass even though the required count or service disappears during the swap. | Unit declarations, commitment/start record, service qualification and TSO dispatch plan. EirGrid/SONI set minimum-unit rules by jurisdiction and separately require inertia. [Operational Policy Roadmap, dynamic stability](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/EirGrid-SONI-Operational-Policy-Roadmap-2025-2035.pdf). |
| **Replace upward or downward reserve with another provider** | **Credited service, not generic MW:** qualification for the exact product/direction, current award or declaration, activation speed, sustained MW, energy/state of charge and any competing service commitment. Is the replacement *credited and deliverable* for the required tranche, without double-counting? | Equal nameplate MW does not mean equal reserve. A provider may respond too late, exhaust its energy or already be assigned to another service. | TSO service awards/declarations and provider telemetry/testing. BMPS requires service capability to be proven and monitored; the 2024 LFC agreement addresses direction, dimensioning and geographic limits. [BMPS v9, §3.2.1](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Balancing_Market_Principles_Statement_Version_9.0.pdf); [LFC Block Operational Agreement, December 2024, Art. 4](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/LFCBOA%20-Load-Frequency-Control-Block-Operational-Agreement-for-Ireland-and-Northern-Ireland.pdf). |
| **Commit an offline unit, storage or demand reduction for an upward ramp** | **Start and sustain feasibility:** earliest start after notice, MW at the relevant one-, three- or eight-hour deadline, then MW/energy maintained for the required period. Can the named resource deliver the whole trajectory, rather than just an eventual peak? | A positive aggregate ramp margin can rely on an offline or energy-limited resource that misses the deadline or cannot hold output. | Unit status, start/ramp declarations and dated dispatch plan. Published RM1/RM3/RM8 define different delivery and hold periods. [EirGrid/SONI ramping-margin note](https://www.sem-o.com/sites/semo/files/documents/general-publications/Ramping_Margin_Requirements_in_Scheduling.pdf). |
| **Use a battery for energy absorption while preserving frequency service** | **Charge-state service conflict:** state of charge, remaining MWh to full, charging MW, upward/downward response headroom after charging and energy reserved for existing commitments. Can the battery absorb energy **and still supply the frequency service credited to it**? | The family balance or SNSP result may pass using charging MW while the action consumes the battery headroom reserved for response. | Storage telemetry, current service award and effective battery dispatch policy. BMPS says storage's state of charge is managed to retain high- and low-frequency response and describes an evolving energy-reserve policy; do not hard-code an old portfolio threshold. [BMPS v9, §4.4.5](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Balancing_Market_Principles_Statement_Version_9.0.pdf). |

## SNSP

| Action candidate in the existing list | Crucial **action-specific** measure and go/no-go question | Why the general SNSP margin is insufficient | Evidence and data owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lower imports or increase exports by interconnector action** | Apply the **agreed feasible transfer** gate above, then recalculate SNSP from the *signed* accepted MW and all other changed components. Is the ratio improvement still present at delivery and return-to-schedule? | A proposed transfer direction can look helpful on paper but be refused, unavailable, too slow or temporary. | TSO/other-TSO agreed schedule and live interconnector telemetry. [BMPS v9, §3.4.6](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Balancing_Market_Principles_Statement_Version_9.0.pdf); [Operational Policy Roadmap, SNSP definition](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/EirGrid-SONI-Operational-Policy-Roadmap-2025-2035.pdf). |
| **Charge storage, pump or add contracted demand while allowing more renewable output** | **Paired numerator/denominator change:** actual added consumption, renewable MW released at the same time, signed transfer changes, storage charge duration and rebound. Does the full recalculated SNSP ratio improve throughout the window? | Added demand alone can lower a ratio; admitting an equal amount of additional non-synchronous generation can reverse that apparent gain. Checking only the new load MW is misleading. | TSO SNSP inputs plus asset/customer metering and agreement. The ratio definition is documented; this paired-action screen is **our inference** from it. [Operational Policy Roadmap, SNSP definition](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/EirGrid-SONI-Operational-Policy-Roadmap-2025-2035.pdf). |
| **All-island WDT renewable limit** | Apply the **WDT instruction and delivered-MW** gate above to the all-island set, then verify the resulting SNSP ratio and any simultaneous local constraint. Are the correct farms limited and did enough MW actually fall? | A safe theoretical ratio assumes delivery from the right units; existing local setpoints and farm response can change the incremental result. | TSO WDT and farm telemetry. EirGrid distinguishes all-island curtailment from constraint groups and describes setpoint allocation. [WDT constraint-group overview, Appendix 1](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Wind-Dispatch-Tool-Constraint-Group-Overview_1.pdf). |

## Operator confirmation and data gap

The **documented** requirements above include equipment and protection limits, dispatch declarations, service qualification, interconnector coordination, outage planning and WDT setpoint allocation. The particular **go/no-go display and transition calculations** are our proposed way to surface them; operators must confirm thresholds, responsible control room, and which checks are mandatory for each action. The [project data guide](DATA_GUIDE.md) has historical national context and reason-code dispatch-down. It does **not** contain the work-party release, breaker/protection state, live site and battery telemetry, service awards, accepted trades or case-linked instruction response needed to mark these extra gates as passed. [Network data feasibility audit](NETWORK_DATA_FEASIBILITY_2026-09-28.md).

**Before offering any of these actions:** obtain a named asset and time-stamped evidence for its extra gate; calculate the combined effect against the current plan and every instruction already in force; require all relevant family-level gates to pass. An unknown extra gate means “needs operator review,” not “safe to enact.”
